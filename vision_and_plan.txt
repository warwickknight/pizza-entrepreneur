# Pizza Tycoon 3D: Stall to Slice — Game Vision & Master Design Document

**Pizza Tycoon 3D** is a mobile-first, arcade-idle management game built for the web that translates tactile restaurant mechanics—walking, stacking, baking, and serving—into an educational simulation of real-world business operations, microeconomics, and entrepreneurship.

```
                    [ 3-Day Forecast & Rota Planning ]
                                    │
                                    ▼
       [ Weather & Payday Modifiers Determine Daily Customer Footfall ]
                                    │
                                    ▼
       [ Customer Queue Arrives ] ◄─── [ Optional: Marketing Campaigns ]
                 │
                 ▼
       [ Take Order at Counter ]  ─── (Chit sent to Kitchen Rail)
                 │
                 ▼
      [ Prep & Fire in Stone Oven ]
                 │
                 ▼
    [ Box Pizza & Stack on Head ]
                 │
                 ▼
     [ Serve & Contactless Checkout ] ── (Card Interchange Fee / Net Revenue)
                 │
                 ▼
    [ End-of-Day P&L: Pay Fixed Wages, Settle COGS, Manage Cash Runway ]
                 │
                 ▼
    [ Reinvest Capital into Payroll Capacity, Equipment & Venue Upgrades ]
```

---

### 1. Core Design Pillars

* **Tactile Arcade-Idle Loop:** Movement feels physical and immediate via a dynamic floating touch joystick (or WASD). Players manage tangible constraints: carry limits, walking distances, and box-stack wobble physics.
* **Realistic Business Simulation (No Arcade Clichés):** Cash does not float mysteriously on the floor. Customers pay at a contactless POS terminal (subject to 2.5% merchant transaction fees) or into a brass till. Financial statements track Gross Revenue, Cost of Goods Sold (COGS), card interchange fees, and payroll.
* **Theory of Constraints & Operational Bottlenecks:** Players physically experience production bottlenecks. Increasing marketing or counter speed causes a backlog if the oven cannot bake fast enough; upgrading the oven creates excess inventory if front-of-house logistics cannot deliver it.
* **Labor Economics & Operating Leverage:** Moving from solo founder-operator (zero payroll, hard personal ceiling on revenue) to managing hired employees (fixed overhead, higher throughput, passive operating profits).
* **Macro Volatility & Forecasting:** Demand is not a static conveyor belt. Footfall is governed by weather conditions, the day of the week, macroeconomic payday cycles, and marketing campaigns, forcing players to forecast before committing to expenses.
* **Ultralight Web Tech Stack:** Built using pure JavaScript, Three.js (low-poly isometric WebGL), and procedural Web Audio API. Requires zero external asset downloads, keeping initial load times under 1 MB.

### 2. Core Economic & Operational Systems

#### A. The Labor Engine: Weekly Rota (Shift Scheduling)
* **The Manager's Clipboard:** Interactive prop on stall opens a 7-day (Monday–Sunday) scheduling grid for hired staff (Chef and Server).
* **Committed Fixed Payroll:** When a worker is scheduled for a day, their shift wage is committed regardless of whether customers show up. Unscheduled staff do not accrue wages; scheduled staff trigger idle animations when quiet.
* **Labor Cost Benchmark:** The end-of-day P&L tracks the real-world operational ratio:
$$\text{Labor Cost Ratio} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Daily Wages Paid}}{\text{Gross Revenue}} \right) \times 100$$
  * *Healthy Benchmark (25%–35%):* Optimal staff utilization and balanced profit margins.
  * *Overstaffed (>55%):* High fixed-cost drag eroding net profit during low-volume shifts.
  * *Understaffed (<15% with walkaways):* Kitchen bottleneck causing queue drop-offs and uncaptured revenue.

#### B. Demand Volatility Engine: Weather & Day-of-Week
Daily footfall is governed by combined operational multipliers:
* **Weekly Rhythm:**
  * *Monday / Tuesday (Slow Slump, 0.5x–0.6x):* Low footfall. Best run solo by the founder to avoid payroll drag.
  * *Wednesday / Thursday (Steady, 0.9x–1.1x):* Moderate traffic. Justifies a single helper (Chef).
  * *Friday Night Rush (1.8x):* Peak demand. High volume requires full staffing (Chef + Server) to prevent queue walkaways.
  * *Saturday Market Day (1.6x):* High traffic with larger group orders.
  * *Sunday (Lazy, 0.7x):* Moderate wind-down shift.
* **Dynamic Weather States:**
  * *Sunny & Warm (1.25x footfall, +20% queue patience):* Ideal outdoor conditions; golden ambient scene lighting.
  * *Overcast (1.0x footfall):* Baseline operations.
  * *Rainy (0.50x footfall, -30% queue patience):* Rain particles, wet cobblestone reflections; pedestrians bypass the stall.
  * *Storm (0.25x footfall, -50% queue patience):* High-risk anomaly; severe financial loss if fully staffed.

#### C. Macroeconomics: The 28-Day Payday Cycle
A full in-game month runs across four 7-day cycles, simulating consumer discretionary spending:
* **Week 4 (Month-End Payday Surge):** Footfall increases by +40%. 30% of customers order **2 boxes** at once (testing carry limits and oven batching). Contactless card tipping (10%–15%) increases, and customers become price-inelastic, allowing menu price increases.
* **Week 1 (Post-Payday Afterglow):** Steady, strong volume with healthy margins.
* **Weeks 2 & 3 (The Mid-Month Squeeze):** Discretionary spending contracts. Footfall drops by -15%, customers become price-sensitive, and margins tighten.

#### D. Growth Marketing vs. Operational Bottlenecks
Players can launch paid promotions from a marketing chalkboard, testing their capacity limits:
* **Local Flyers ($15, 45s duration):** Low-risk +25% footfall boost for neighborhood awareness.
* **Social Media Hype ($40, 30s duration):** High-impact +75% footfall blitz with +10% tip probability.
* **"2-for-1" Promo Coupon ($10, 45s duration):** High volume (+120% footfall), but unit price drops from $16 to $11, demonstrating margin compression and loss-leader dynamics.
* **The Marketing Trap:** Launching an ad campaign without sufficient oven or delivery capacity causes queues to spill over. Customers walk away frustrated, leading to wasted ad spend and a negative Return on Ad Spend (ROAS).
