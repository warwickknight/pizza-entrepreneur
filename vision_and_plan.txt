# Pizza Tycoon 3D: Stall to Slice — Game Vision & Master Design Document

**Pizza Tycoon 3D** is a mobile-first, arcade-idle management game built for the web that translates tactile restaurant mechanics—walking, stacking, baking, and serving—into an educational simulation of real-world business operations, microeconomics, and entrepreneurship.

```
                    [ 3-Day Forecast & Rota Planning ]
                                    │
                                    ▼
       [ Weather & Payday Modifiers Determine Daily Customer Footfall ]
                                    │
                                    ▼
       [ Customer Queue Arrives ] ◄─── [ Optional: Marketing Campaigns ]
                 │
                 ▼
       [ Take Order at Counter ]  ─── (Chit sent to Kitchen Rail)
                 │
                 ▼
      [ Prep & Fire in Stone Oven ]
                 │
                 ▼
    [ Box Pizza & Stack on Head ]
                 │
                 ▼
     [ Serve & Contactless Checkout ] ── (Card Interchange Fee / Net Revenue)
                 │
                 ▼
    [ End-of-Day P&L: Pay Fixed Wages, Settle COGS, Manage Cash Runway ]
                 │
                 ▼
    [ Reinvest Capital into Payroll Capacity, Equipment & Venue Upgrades ]

```

---

### 1. Core Design Pillars

* **Tactile Arcade-Idle Loop:** Movement feels physical and immediate via a dynamic floating touch joystick (or WASD). Players manage tangible constraints: carry limits, walking distances, and box-stack wobble physics.
* **Realistic Business Simulation (No Arcade Clichés):** Cash does not float mysteriously on the floor. Customers pay at a contactless POS terminal (subject to 2.5% merchant transaction fees) or into a brass till. Financial statements track Gross Revenue, Cost of Goods Sold (COGS), card interchange fees, and payroll.
* **Theory of Constraints & Operational Bottlenecks:** Players physically experience production bottlenecks. Increasing marketing or counter speed causes a backlog if the oven cannot bake fast enough; upgrading the oven creates excess inventory if front-of-house logistics cannot deliver it.
* **Labor Economics & Operating Leverage:** Moving from solo founder-operator (zero payroll, hard personal ceiling on revenue) to managing hired employees (fixed overhead, higher throughput, passive operating profits).
* **Macro Volatility & Forecasting:** Demand is not a static conveyor belt. Footfall is governed by weather conditions, the day of the week, macroeconomic payday cycles, and marketing campaigns, forcing players to forecast before committing to expenses.
* **Ultralight Web Tech Stack:** Built using pure JavaScript, Three.js (low-poly isometric WebGL), and procedural Web Audio API. Requires zero external asset downloads, keeping initial load times under 1 MB.

### 2. Core Economic & Operational Systems

#### A. The Labor Engine: Weekly Rota (Shift Scheduling)

* **The Manager's Clipboard:** An interactive prop inside the stall opens a 7-day (Monday–Sunday) scheduling grid for hired staff (Chef and Server).
* **Committed Fixed Payroll:** When a worker is scheduled for a day, their shift wage is committed regardless of whether customers show up. If a player schedules staff on a slow, rainy day, workers stand idle (with bored animations) while their wage accrues.
* **Labor Cost Benchmark:** The end-of-day P&L tracks the real-world operational ratio:

$$\text{Labor Cost Ratio} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Daily Wages Paid}}{\text{Gross Revenue}} \right) \times 100$$


* *Healthy Benchmark (25%–35%):* Optimal staff utilization and balanced profit margins.
* *Overstaffed (>55%):* High fixed-cost drag eroding net profit during low-volume shifts.
* *Understaffed (<15% with walkaways):* Kitchen bottleneck causing queue drop-offs and uncaptured revenue.



#### B. Demand Volatility Engine: Weather & Day-of-Week

Daily footfall is governed by combined operational multipliers:

* **Weekly Rhythm:**
* *Monday / Tuesday (Slow Slump, 0.5x–0.6x):* Low footfall. Best run solo by the founder to avoid payroll drag.
* *Wednesday / Thursday (Steady, 0.9x–1.1x):* Moderate traffic. Justifies a single helper (Chef).
* *Friday Night Rush (1.8x):* Peak demand. High volume requires full staffing (Chef + Server) to prevent queue walkaways.
* *Saturday Market Day (1.6x):* High traffic with larger group orders.
* *Sunday (Lazy, 0.7x):* Moderate wind-down shift.


* **Dynamic Weather States:**
* *Sunny & Warm (1.25x footfall, +20% queue patience):* Ideal outdoor conditions; golden ambient scene lighting.
* *Overcast (1.0x footfall):* Baseline operations.
* *Rainy (0.50x footfall, -30% queue patience):* Rain particles, wet cobblestone reflections; pedestrians bypass the stall.
* *Storm (0.25x footfall, -50% queue patience):* High-risk anomaly; severe financial loss if fully staffed.



#### C. Macroeconomics: The 28-Day Payday Cycle

A full in-game month runs across four 7-day cycles, simulating consumer discretionary spending:

* **Week 4 (Month-End Payday Surge):** Footfall increases by +40%. 30% of customers order **2 boxes** at once (testing carry limits and oven batching). Contactless card tipping (10%–15%) increases, and customers become price-inelastic, allowing menu price increases.
* **Week 1 (Post-Payday Afterglow):** Steady, strong volume with healthy margins.
* **Weeks 2 & 3 (The Mid-Month Squeeze):** Discretionary spending contracts. Footfall drops by -15%, customers become price-sensitive, and margins tighten.

#### D. Growth Marketing vs. Operational Bottlenecks

Players can launch paid promotions from a marketing chalkboard, testing their capacity limits:

* **Local Flyers ($15, 45s duration):** Low-risk +25% footfall boost for neighborhood awareness.
* **Social Media Hype ($40, 30s duration):** High-impact +75% footfall blitz with +10% tip probability.
* **"2-for-1" Promo Coupon ($10, 45s duration):** High volume (+120% footfall), but unit price drops from $16 to $11, demonstrating margin compression and loss-leader dynamics.
* **The Marketing Trap:** Launching an ad campaign without sufficient oven or delivery capacity causes queues to spill over. Customers walk away frustrated, leading to wasted ad spend and a negative Return on Ad Spend (ROAS).

### 3. Current Prototype State (The Baseline)

The existing prototype (`pizza_tycoon_3d.html`) provides the foundational proof of concept:

* **Environment:** Fully visible, open-air cobblestone market terrace with an active stone pizza oven, animated hearth fire, prep counter with rolling pin, ticket rack, boxed pickup table, front counter, and street curb.
* **Manual Operations Pipeline:** Take orders at the counter $\rightarrow$ knead and fire dough at the stone oven with circular radial feedback $\rightarrow$ box pizzas onto the prep table $\rightarrow$ stack finished boxes on the player's head $\rightarrow$ deliver to counter.
* **Realistic Checkout:** Contactless POS terminal (audio beeps, deductions of 2.5% card interchange fees) and a brass cash till.
* **Hired Staff Automation:** Distinct upgrade pads for **Chef ($45)** (automates cooking) and **Server ($60)** (automates order taking and delivery) with debounced shortfall prompts.
* **Live P&L Statement:** Real-time breakdown of Gross Revenue, COGS ($3.50/pizza), Card Interchange Fees (2.5%), Staff Payroll, and Net Operating Profit.

### 4. Comprehensive Development Roadmap

```
PHASE 1 ──────► PHASE 2 ──────► PHASE 3 ──────► PHASE 4 ──────► PHASE 5
Stall Polish    Labor Rota,     Supply Chain    Work-Life       Venue Upgrades
& Persistence   Demand & Ads    & Pricing       & Dilemmas      & Distribution

```

#### Phase 1: Stall Polish, Feedback & Persistence

* **Data Persistence:** Implement `localStorage` schema saving bank balance, hired staff, lifetime stats, and inventory across browser reloads.
* **Visual Polish & Character Animation:** Add leg-swing locomotion, dough-tossing animations at the prep table, and procedural smoke/steam particles rising from the stone oven chimney.
* **Customer Archetypes:**
* *Impatient Commuter:* Short patience timer, card-only, high tip for fast service.
* *Family / Group:* Orders 2–3 pizzas, waits longer, tests carry stack capacity.
* *Frugal Local:* Cash-only, highly sensitive to price increases.


* **Tactile Haptics:** Integrate the Web Vibration API (`navigator.vibrate`) for subtle physical clicks during contactless card taps, box pickups, and upgrade completions.

#### Phase 2: Labor Scheduling, Volatility & Marketing

* **Manager's Rota Clipboard:**
* Add a physical clipboard prop beside the till to open the 7-day (Mon–Sun) staffing grid.
* Toggle Chef and Server schedules to balance labor expense against forecasted volume.
* Unscheduled staff despawn for the day; scheduled staff collect wages and trigger idle animations on quiet days.


* **Dynamic Demand Engine:**
* Implement the 7-day footfall cycle (Monday slump to Friday dinner rush).
* Add a 3-day weather and payday forecast widget to the clipboard to inform scheduling decisions.
* Implement weather visuals (falling rain line particles, cloudy skybox shifts, wet surface shaders).


* **Payday Cycle Mechanics:**
* 28-day monthly calendar with Week 4 payday spikes, multi-box orders, and increased card tipping.


* **Marketing Campaigns:**
* Deploy the marketing board for Local Flyers, Social Blitzes, and 2-for-1 Coupons.
* Track walkaways and calculate Campaign ROAS directly on the daily debrief screen.



#### Phase 3: Supply Chain, Recipes & Pricing Elasticity

* **Raw Ingredient Inventory:**
* Split COGS into tangible stock items: Flour/Dough Balls, Tomato Sauce, and Mozzarella Blocks.
* Restock via a delivery pallet pad: choose between *Daily Small Orders* (high unit cost) vs. *Wholesale Pallets* (low unit cost, high working-capital requirement).


* **Spoilage & Cold Storage:**
* Perishable cheese spoils overnight unless the player invests in a refrigerated counter upgrade.


* **Menu Pricing Slider:**
* Allow setting pizza prices between $10 and $26.
* Dynamically calculate demand elasticity: underpricing creates unmanageable queues; overpricing reduces foot traffic while boosting unit gross margins.



#### Phase 4: Work-Life Balance & Founder Dilemmas

* **Stamina & Burnout Meter:**
* Operating continuous manual shifts drains the founder's stamina bar, slowing walk speed and increasing preparation errors.
* Delegating shifts to hired staff allows the founder to rest and recover stamina.


* **Personal Life & Morale Tracker:**
* Introduce an off-shift family morale meter.
* Trigger narrative dilemma events at the end of shifts:
* *Example:* "Your daughter's school play coincides with Friday's payday rush. Do you close early (-$140 revenue, +25 family morale) or stay open to cover supplier debt (+revenue, -family morale)?"




* **Working Capital Loans:** Introduce bank micro-loans ($250 at 8% daily interest) to fund early equipment or marketing, teaching debt service coverage and interest risk.

#### Phase 5: Venue Progression & Production Deployment

* **Venue Scaling:**
* *Tier 1: Street Stall (Baseline):* Hand-operated market stand with a single stone oven.
* *Tier 2: Mobile Food Truck:* Unlocks mobility to festival/beach zones, vehicle drive-up order lanes, generator gas costs, and street permit overhead.
* *Tier 3: Brick-and-Mortar Pizzeria:* Dine-in seating, plate-washing mechanics, delivery moped drivers, dual stone ovens, and a dedicated back office.


* **PWA & Production Optimization:**
* Web App Manifest and Service Worker configuration for full-screen, offline-capable mobile home-screen installation.
* Mesh instancing (`InstancedMesh`) for crowded customer queues and pizza box stacks to maintain a locked 60 FPS on low-power mobile devices.


* **Educational Export:** Add an end-of-game performance review scoring Return on Investment (ROI), Labor Efficiency Ratio, Working Capital Management, and Work-Life Equilibrium.
